Research

My research interests grow out of problems I meet in practice: how can an organisation describe what its information means, who may use it, what evidence supports it, and how those promises survive changes in technology and teams?

I am developing an independent research agenda rather than presenting a publication record. The public DataGovOps and DataGovSecOps repositories contain early models, schemas, examples, and questions. They are proposals to test, not settled standards or legal guidance.

Questions I am exploring

  • Information products and data products: What should be specified independently of a particular platform or dataset, and what belongs to each technical realization?
  • Executable governance: Which expectations can be expressed in machine-readable form and checked during delivery, and which still require human judgment?
  • Evidence over assertions: How should provenance, purpose, authority, validity, and change history be recorded so that a decision can be understood later?
  • Security and assurance: How can policy evidence and control ownership follow a data product through its lifecycle without turning governance into a detached paperwork exercise?

I want this work to be useful to practitioners and open to critique. A model is only interesting if it can be applied, challenged with counterexamples, and revised in the light of evidence.

Research notes will appear here as they are ready. I will label hypotheses, implementations, and evaluated findings separately.

Areas I am learning about

AI raises practical governance questions about provenance, access, accountability, and what a system can actually be trusted to do. I am also interested in cybersecurity and post-quantum cryptography. These are areas of active learning for me, not specialist credentials or claims of research results. I expect to learn through public sources, working examples, and conversations with practitioners who know them well.
